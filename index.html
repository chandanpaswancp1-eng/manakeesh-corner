The pantry

Levantine spice blends.

Two families: a green, sour, seedy finish — and a warm ground mix for meat. Zaatar is a topping. Seven-spice is a seasoning. They are not interchangeable.

Finish

Zaatar

Dried Syrian oregano, toasted sesame, sumac and salt. Stirred with olive oil into a paste, then dimpled into the dough so the oil stays put.

Seasoning

Seven-spice

Sabaa baharat — the Lebanese warm blend for lahm b ajin. Allspice, cinnamon, pepper and clove at the core; cumin, coriander and nutmeg fill the rest.

Herb, plant, and blend

Zaatar is the wild plant, the dried herb, and the table mix. On manakeesh we use the mix — never a dry dusting.

Sumac

Ground dried berries. Lemon-red. Fattoush, onions, labneh, and the sour in zaatar.

Aleppo / shatta

Oily, fruity chili. Heat without covering the herb.

Mahlab

Ground cherry-pit kernel. Almond-bitter, for kaak and sweet breads.

Mastic

Tree resin. A few grains in bread, ice cream, sometimes meat.

Nigella

Black seed on kaak and cheese fateera.

Kishk

Fermented dried yogurt and burghul. Crumbled over manakeesh — sour, dairy, cereal.
